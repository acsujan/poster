# ASNEngr 2026 — Submission Abstract

**Title:** Wearable sEMG fatigue monitoring on roofers in hot environments

**Authors:** Sujan Acharya and Krishna P. Kisi, Ph.D., P.E., Texas State University

## Abstract

Nepali migrants build much of the Gulf, roofing through summer afternoons, with musculoskeletal disorders carried home rather than recorded. Muscle fatigue precedes these disorders and the falls that injure roofers. Surface electromyography (sEMG) can track fatigue before a worker feels it, so a body-worn sensor could warn in time; however, nearly all evidence for such sensors comes from climate-controlled laboratories. This study ran one roofing protocol in two settings: indoors on a 30° roof platform (13 participants) and outdoors on a 30° roof mock-up at 33–39 °C air temperature (9 roofing workers), using ten sEMG channels from the lower back to the calf. A channel counted as usable only if it cleared the sensor's noise floor and its own resting level and kept a median frequency within the 50–150 Hz muscle band. Nine of ten channels were usable in the laboratory but only four outdoors, and the lower-back resting baseline doubled (9 to 19 µV, p = 0.001). Of 20 possible muscle-and-posture comparisons, only 8 remained interpretable. A fatigue model trained one worker at a time reached 49% accuracy outdoors, below the 76% scored by always guessing the majority class. Heat and sweat removed the leg channels first while the trunk channels largely held, so sensor placement decides whether a field study measures fatigue or noise. Field sEMG studies should place lower-back channels first, verify every channel live before recording, and report which channels survived. No such roof-worn data exist for Nepali workers; these lessons are a starting point.

## Keywords

surface electromyography (sEMG); muscle fatigue; roofing; heat exposure; wearable sensors; construction safety; field validation; Nepali migrant workers
